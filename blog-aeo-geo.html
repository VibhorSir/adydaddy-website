How to Get Your D2C Brand Cited by AI Search Engines

By Vibhor Sharma Sep 22, 2026
Short answer: AI search engines cite content that directly answers questions with clear, authoritative, structured information. To get cited, publish direct-answer content, add structured data, provide an llms.txt, and earn citations from authoritative sources.

Search is shifting from ten blue links to direct answers. When someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "what is a good ROAS for D2C in India," the engine pulls from sources it trusts. Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) are how you become that source.

1. Publish direct answers, not essays

AI engines reward content that states the answer immediately, then explains. Put a clear "short answer" up top, then support it. Structure with headings that match real questions. This is the single biggest lever, and it is also good for humans.

2. Use structured data

Schema markup (Article, FAQPage, Organization, Person) tells machines what your content is about in a language they parse perfectly. FAQPage schema in particular helps your answers surface in AI Overviews and answer boxes.

3. Provide an llms.txt

An llms.txt file gives AI crawlers a clean, machine-readable summary of your site: what you do, your key pages, and your content. It is the AEO equivalent of a sitemap, and it makes it easy for engines to understand and cite you.

4. Be the original source

AI engines prefer the original, authoritative source over a copy. Publish real data, real benchmarks, and real case studies that others then cite. When other sites and forums link to you as the reference, the engines follow.

5. Cover the long tail of questions

The questions people ask AI are specific: "why does my ROAS drop when I scale," "how to lower CAC without cutting budget." Publishing content that answers these exact questions, one per page, is how you get picked up for a wide range of queries.

What to do first

Start with your FAQ pages and your highest-intent questions. Make every answer direct and citable. Add the schema. Ship an llms.txt. Then publish one direct-answer article at a time. GEO compounds slowly, but the sources that win are the ones that answered clearly, first.
