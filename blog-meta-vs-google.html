Meta Ads vs Google Ads for D2C: Where to Spend First

By Vibhor Sharma Sep 22, 2026
Short answer: If people already search for your category, start with Google Ads (demand capture). If you sell something people do not know they need, start with Meta Ads (demand creation). Most D2C brands eventually need both, but the order matters.

The "Meta vs Google" debate is usually framed wrong. They are not competing channels; they solve different problems. One captures demand that already exists, the other creates demand that did not. Understanding that distinction is the whole answer.

Demand capture vs demand creation

Google Ads (Search and Shopping) captures intent. Someone types "best running shoes for flat feet" and Google shows your product. The demand is already there; you are paying to be the one who catches it. Conversion rates are high because the person is already looking to buy.

Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) create demand. Nobody opens Instagram looking for your product. You interrupt their scroll, stop them with a hook, and build enough desire that they click and buy. The demand is created from nothing, so conversion rates are lower, but the ceiling is much higher.

When to start with Google Ads

Start with Google when your category has existing search volume. Obvious cases: products people actively look for (supplements, electronics, replacement parts, category-specific queries). If people type your product type into Google every day, you can capture that demand on day one without educating anyone.

Google is also the safer first bet when your margins are thin, because you are paying for clicks that already have intent, so efficiency comes faster.

When to start with Meta Ads

Start with Meta when you sell something people do not know exists, or something driven by visual appeal and impulse. New categories, fashion, gifting, novelty products, and anything where a scroll-stopping video is the best salesman. Meta is also the channel that scales creative, so if you have strong video assets, it can compound fast.

How to split budget as you scale

StageGoogleMetaWhy
Launch60-70%30-40%Capture existing demand first, prove efficiency
Growth40-50%50-60%Meta creates new demand as Google plateaus
Scale30-40%60-70%Meta is where the ceiling is; Google defends the base

These are starting points, not rules. The real answer always comes from your numbers: follow the channel that returns above your break-even ROAS, and rebalance monthly.

The mistake most brands make

They treat both channels as interchangeable and split budget 50/50 from day one. That dilutes learning. Pick a primary channel based on intent, prove it works, then layer the second one on top. One channel done well beats two done badly.
